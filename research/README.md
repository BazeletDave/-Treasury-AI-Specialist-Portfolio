# Institutional AI Governance | Living Research Index

Grand Research Institute | Established 21 October 2026

This research environment extends the original Treasury AI Specialist portfolio into a longitudinal study of institutional AI adoption, accessibility, delegated authority, and governance. The original employment portfolio and deployment remain historical artifacts, not evidence of Treasury sponsorship.

## Research streams

| Stream | Central question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institutional accessibility | Who can independently build institutional capabilities? | Reproducible prototypes, documented boundaries |
| Delegation and agency | Which decisions can AI recommend or execute? | Authorization matrices, human approvals, logs |
| Cybersecurity and resilience | How do tools, models, and integrations alter institutional exposure? | Official advisories, threat models, mitigations |
| Regulatory governance | How do formal rules adapt to AI capabilities? | Primary agency publications, laws, standards |
| Algorithmic management | How do automated decisions change managerial authority and incentives? | Observations, validated operational cases |
| Institutional legitimacy | How are decisions explained, contested and remediated? | Audit trails, review procedures, accountability |

## Institutional analysis

Apply the Nelson Model as an analytical lens: formal rules, informal practices, actors, interests, power, implementation, legitimacy and institutional adaptation. This is an analytical framework, not a claim of endorsement by an academic institution.

## Evidence standard

Every case must identify primary sources, publication and access dates, the nature of evidence, observed versus inferred claims, limitations, and authorization boundaries. Agency warnings establish the contents of an advisory, not proof that a specific system was compromised. Research findings are not certification.
