# GRI-IA-004 | Institutional Accessibility and Software Assurance: Cross Case Synthesis

**Opened:** 2026-10-21  
**Classification:** Research hypothesis and comparative design, not validated causal finding

## Thesis

Accessible AI development enables independent construction of institutionally relevant capabilities. Institutional adoption also depends on secure software supply chains, authorization, operational controls and human accountability. The combined governance problem spans technical capability, institutional legitimacy and operational resilience.

## Cases

- [GRI-IA-001: Treasury TTB prototype](https://github.com/BazeletDave/Treasury-TTB-Label-Verification): simulated regulatory workflow, independent prototype, no Treasury integration.
- [GRI-IA-002: Treasury AI Specialist portfolio](../../README.md): publicly documented governance portfolio and demonstration application.
- [GRI-IA-003: PHP software assurance](GRI-IA-003-PHP-SOFTWARE-ASSURANCE.md): case opened for verified advisories, runtime evidence and patch validation.

## Testable propositions

**P1:** Reduced development barriers increase the number of independently developed tools capable of imitating institutional functions.

**P2:** Institutional risk is conditioned by authorization, data access, supply chain integrity, and decision authority rather than prototype capability alone.

**P3:** Evidence based control validation reduces uncertainty about operational assurance, but does not independently establish institutional legitimacy.

**P4:** Longitudinal documentation of exceptions, corrective actions and authority boundaries improves the reproducibility of governance assessments.

## Comparative measurement plan

Track source quality, authorization level, software provenance, validation coverage, human review requirements, observed failures, remediation and institutional impact. Clearly distinguish measured observations from hypotheses.
